## Source-document discrepancies preserved

1. The SOP’s internal Version 1.3 header is a **known typographical error**. The file name and Version 1.4 change history establish the controlling revision per the user’s instruction.
2. The SOP specifies an internal-standard preparation of 5.0 µL dimethylacetamide brought to 25 mL with ethyl acetate. The Form records 5.0 µL plus 4,995 µL, a 5 mL total. The SOP controls; the Form entry is **stale** pending controlled-document correction.
3. The SOP footer text references a Residual Solvents SOP and its structural page indicators do not agree with the title table. This is **stale** source-document footer metadata, separate from the known Version 1.3 typo.
4. The Form’s structural page indicators also disagree. Because page rendering was unavailable, the physical-page layout is **Unable to verify read-only**.
5. The Form summarizes blank suitability more generally than the SOP and omits the SOP’s resolution and peak-area applicability criteria. The SOP controls.
6. The SOP references LCS injections in bracketing frequency, while no separately labeled LCS-preparation block was identified in the Form. The intended record and relationship are **Unable to verify read-only** and must not be invented.

## Cannabinoid Potency protocol reconciliation

Cannabinoid Potency protocol ID 4 is the strongest visible production structural reference for a future Terpenes protocol, but it is not a gold standard and must not be cloned blindly.

- Protocol 4 has 24 ordered assignments and covers resource/equipment assignment, solution preparation, several sample-preparation segments, dilution, instrumental analysis, quality control, result upload, and final review.
- The captured UI exposes a flat ordered list. Entry conditions, required/optional state, branching, and failure behavior were not exposed.
- Worksheet 39 has an active approved definition associated with protocol-step definition 21, but that step is not assigned to protocol 4. The calculation/reporting segment is therefore incomplete in the visible ordered protocol.
- Step definition 20 is an unassigned duplicate-name/same-worksheet counterpart to assigned step 19; their descriptions/SOP references differ, so they are not treated as exact duplicates.
- The last assigned protocol-4 step uses shared worksheet 41. Worksheet 41 is active but has zero versions and no versioned worksheet definition available; it is reused by 12 protocols.
- Patch step definitions 74–76 are also unassigned. Their existence does not prove that protocol 4 uses them.
- Terpenes protocol 9 has zero steps and assay 8 exposes no protocol assignment, so there is no current Terpenes sequence to reconcile against the SOP.

A future Terpenes protocol design should be reviewed as distinct method segments rather than copied IDs: resource/equipment assignment; internal standard and calibration/QC solution preparation; matrix spike; matrix-specific sample preparation; run-sequence setup; instrument acquisition; integration/calculation review; QC acceptance/failure handling; result import and parser review; report-range/pass-fail review; and final approval. No such QBench changes are authorized or performed by this scan.

## End-to-end dependency assessment

The currently evidenced production chain is:

`SOP/Form authority outside QBench` → `assay 8` → `parser 50` → `BW43 Results` → `automation 17` → `TW42` → `report 26`

That chain transfers instrument-result data, but it does not establish the full controlled method. Missing or unverified layers include panel membership, assigned protocol and steps, method value, resource group, Terpenes controls/control group, inventory/equipment assignments, preparation and QC records, current worksheet formulas/data types, current `report_results` and `pass_fail` names, specification behavior, and failure/review workflow.

## Phase 6 conclusion

Terpenes is **Partially represented** in production QBench. The parser-to-report data path is materially developed, but the controlling SOP is not represented as an assigned, reviewable protocol and the current worksheet definitions could not be exported. The highest-priority safe next step is a separate authorized Sandbox design and validation cycle, preceded by controlled correction of the Form/SOP discrepancies and followed by a read-only export verification. This production scan made zero QBench mutations.
